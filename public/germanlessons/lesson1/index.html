Step 1

Your first sentence

A good German sentence can be just subject + verb + object. The verb sits in the middle, just like in English.

SubjektIch
Verb / Prädikatlerne
ObjektDeutsch.

= I learn German.

Some books say Prädikat instead of Verb. Technically one is a sentence part and one is a function, but for now: it's almost the same thing. Don't stress.
Step 2

The infinitive: stem + -en

Verbs in the dictionary form (the infinitive) almost all end in -en. Cut off the ending and you have the stem.

InfinitiveStemEndingMeaning
lernenlern--ento learn
kommenkomm--ento come
konjugierenkonjugier--ento conjugate
Step 3

Personal pronouns (Nominativ)

The person doing the action. Today only the first case: Nominativ.

GermanEnglish
ichI
duyou (one person, informal)
er / sie / eshe / she / it
manone, people in general
wirwe
ihryou guys (plural, informal)
siethey
Sieyou (polite), always with a capital S
man ≠ the man! Man spricht in Deutschland Deutsch. = One speaks German in Germany. It makes a general statement, nobody specific.
Step 4 · the most important part

Conjugation: the endings

Take the stem and add the ending that matches the person.

PronounEndinglernen
ich-elerneIch lerne Deutsch.
du-stlernstDon't forget the t!
er / sie / es / man-tlerntEr lernt Deutsch.
wir-enlernenWir lernen Deutsch.
ihr-tlerntIhr lernt Deutsch.
sie / Sie-enlernenSie lernen Deutsch.
Watch out for "Sie" at the start of a sentence. Every sentence starts with a capital letter, so Sie can be she, they or polite you. The verb ending helps:
Sie kauft Cola. → she buys a Coke (-t)
Sie kaufen Cola. → they buy / you (polite) buy a Coke (-en)
Step 5

Two little words: auch & oder

auch = also, too

Er lernt Deutsch. Ich lerne auch Deutsch.He learns German. I also learn German.

Auch ich lerne Deutsch.Same meaning, but it puts the emphasis on "I".

Ich wohne in Berlin. Wohnst du auch in Berlin?I live in Berlin. Do you live in Berlin too?

Handy everyday phrase: "… ist auch okay" = "… is also fine"
Wasser ist auch okay. Water is also fine.
Montag ist auch okay. Monday is fine with me too.

oder = or … / right?

Trinkst du Kaffee oder Tee?Do you drink coffee or tea? (A or B)

Du lernst Deutsch, oder?You learn German, right? (rhetorical question, "…or not?")

Du bist Amerikaner, oder?You are American, aren't you?
